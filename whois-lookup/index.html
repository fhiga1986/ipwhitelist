What is a WHOIS lookup?

WHOIS is a public database that stores registration information for every domain name on the internet. When someone registers a domain, their contact information, registration date, expiry date, and nameservers are recorded in the WHOIS database. This information is publicly accessible to anyone who wants to look it up.

Technically there is no single database: each registry (Verisign for .com, PIR for .org, Google Registry for .dev…) publishes its own records, and this tool asks the right one for you through RDAP, the modern successor of the WHOIS protocol.

Understanding domain status codes

The Status line uses EPP codes defined by ICANN. Codes starting with client were set by the registrar (usually at the owner's request); codes starting with server were set by the registry and take precedence.

StatusMeaning
ok / activeNo restrictions. Healthy, but also unlocked — most registrars recommend enabling the transfer lock.
clientTransferProhibitedTransfer lock is on: the domain cannot be moved to another registrar without unlocking it first. Normal and recommended.
clientDeleteProhibited / clientUpdateProhibitedDeletion or contact changes are blocked at the registrar level — a standard protection bundle.
clientHold / serverHoldThe domain is suspended and removed from DNS: unverified contact email, legal dispute or abuse. The site and email stop working.
autoRenewPeriodThe domain expired and was auto-renewed by the registry; the registrar has up to 45 days to confirm or cancel the renewal.
redemptionPeriodThe domain was deleted after expiry. The former owner can still restore it for about 30 days, usually for a fee of USD 80–200.
pendingDeleteRestoration is no longer possible; the domain will be released to the public in about 5 days.
pendingTransferA transfer to another registrar is in progress (normally completes within 5 days).

Why use a WHOIS lookup tool?

Developers use WHOIS to verify domain ownership before whitelisting, check if a domain is about to expire, investigate suspicious traffic sources, or confirm nameserver configuration during DNS migrations. Combined with our DNS Lookup tool, WHOIS gives you a complete picture of any domain's configuration.

Other everyday uses: checking whether a domain you want is really taken and when it expires, confirming that a phishing look-alike was registered last week rather than years ago, and finding the registrar's abuse contact to report a malicious site.

What is RDAP and how is it different from WHOIS?

RDAP (Registration Data Access Protocol) is the modern replacement for the traditional WHOIS protocol. While WHOIS returns plain text in inconsistent formats depending on the registrar, RDAP returns structured JSON data that is easier to parse programmatically. RDAP also supports authentication, allowing registrars to provide different levels of detail based on the requester.

This tool uses the RDAP protocol to retrieve domain registration data, which means results are more consistent and reliable than traditional WHOIS lookups that vary by registrar. ICANN made RDAP mandatory for all generic TLDs (.com, .net, .org, .dev, .io, .app and every "new" TLD) and retired the WHOIS port-43 requirement in January 2025. Many country-code TLDs (for example .de, .es, .pe and .jp) still run their own WHOIS services without RDAP, so lookups for those domains may fail here — use the registry's own website instead.

How to protect your WHOIS information

By default, domain registration information (name, email, phone, address) is publicly visible in WHOIS records. To protect your privacy, enable WHOIS privacy protection (also called domain privacy or ID Shield) through your registrar — most registrars offer this for free or a small fee.

With privacy protection enabled, your personal contact details are replaced with the registrar's proxy information in public WHOIS records. Since GDPR took effect in 2018, most registrars redact personal data for everyone by default, which is why the Registrant field often shows "REDACTED FOR PRIVACY" or the name of a privacy service. Registrars are still required to hold accurate information and to disclose it to law enforcement and courts upon legitimate request.

WHOIS lookup questions

Many domain registrars offer WHOIS privacy protection (also called domain privacy or WHOIS guard). When enabled, the registrar's contact details replace the owner's personal information in the public WHOIS database. Since GDPR, most registrars also redact personal data by default. This is common practice to protect domain owners from spam and unwanted contact.
Enter the domain in the WHOIS lookup tool above. If the owner hasn't enabled privacy protection, you'll see their name and organization. If privacy is enabled, you'll see the registrar's proxy information instead; most privacy services forward messages to the real owner, and registrars disclose the data to courts and law enforcement for ownership disputes.
WHOIS data is provided directly by domain registrars and is generally accurate for technical details like nameservers, registration dates, and expiry dates. Contact information may be outdated if the domain owner didn't update it, or hidden behind privacy protection services.
This tool uses RDAP, which every generic TLD supports but only some country-code registries have adopted. If the registry for a ccTLD such as .de, .es or .pe has no RDAP service, there is nothing for the tool to query and it reports an error. Look the domain up on the registry's own website (DENIC, Red.es, NIC.pe) in that case.
For most generic TLDs the owner gets a grace period of up to 45 days to renew at the normal price, then about 30 days of redemption where restoring costs a penalty fee, and finally a 5-day pendingDelete window before the name is released to anyone. The Status field above shows which stage a domain is in.
